# Flint Township (Charter Township of Flint), research notes

Read 2026-11-21 unless noted. A separate government from the City of Flint. Every fact used on the Flint Township pages traces to a line here.

## Government and roads
- "Flint Township roads and secondary roads in the township are maintained by Genesee County Road Commission unless they are State roads which are maintained by MDOT." https://www.flinttownship.org/community/road_construction.php
- Township hall: 1490 S. Dye Road, (810) 732-1350. Same site.
- Genesee County Road Commission (GCRC) Winter Maintenance Policy: https://www.gcrc.org/forms-documents/forms/policies/240-winter-maintenance-policy/file
  - Class 1 state trunklines and multi-lane primaries: first priority, bare pavement. Class 2 and 3 primaries next. Class 4 remainder of primary and local roads: third priority, snow covered with plowing during regular hours only. Class 5 subdivisions and local gravel roads: fourth priority, "snow covered with plowing and grading", no overtime authorized.
  - In blizzards, crews plow major routes until reasonably safe, then move to subdivision and unpaved roads.
  - "Please give 24 hours for Main roads to be plowed and 48 hours for local roads. We cannot give specific times for specific streets."
  - "Why is my driveway plowed in with snow? ... snow is pushed into driveways. This is unavoidable."
  - Cars obstructing snow clearing "will be dealt with under township by-laws as appropriate and may involve towing."
- GCRC FAQ: https://www.gcrc.org/about/frequently-asked-questions
  - Plowing order: expressways, then main streets, then local roads; "Subdivision streets are the final priority." GCRC is responsible for more than 1,600 miles of roads.
  - Mailboxes can be damaged in heavy snow by plowing and scraping; owners are encouraged to inspect mailboxes and posts before winter.
  - Rocks placed along the road are usually in the public right of way; a permit is required to install anything in the right of way. Solid garden borders of decorative timbers, mailboxes on big brick, concrete or steel posts, fences, trees and bushes near the right of way line can be hazards and can cause sight distance problems at intersections and driveways.
- Sidewalk snow ordinance: the township code is on Municode (library.municode.com/mi/flint_charter_township,_(genesee_co.)/codes/code_of_ordinances), which loads by script and could not be read. Pages say "check with the township" and state no rule.

## State law
- MCL 257.677a(4): "A person shall not deposit, or cause to be deposited, snow, ice or slush on any roadway or highway." (3) bars depositing snow onto or across a roadway or its shoulder in a way that obstructs a driver's "safety vision" (an unobstructed line of sight to travel upon, enter or exit a roadway). https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-257-677a

## Leaves, yard waste, burning
- Township recycle page: "Emterra Environmental USA will provide compost pick up to all residents of Flint Township. Compost will be picked up weekly. Please have compost placed curbside by 7am on Day of Service. Service will run April through November." https://www.flinttownship.org/community/trash_recycling_/recycle.php
- Waste, Recycling and Compost Brochure (Emterra), https://www.flinttownship.org/Documents/Community/Trash%20And%20Recycling/WasteRecyclingBrochureFina.pdf :
  - "Your pick up day is THURSDAY." Out by 7:00 a.m.
  - Compost runs "from the first Thursday in April until the last Friday in November".
  - Yard waste in brown yard waste bags or plastic 30 gallon containers clearly marked "X" facing the street. Branches no more than 2 inches in diameter and no longer than 4 feet, bundled. Bundles and containers under 50 pounds. No stumps or trunks. No compost in plastic bags.
  - Unacceptable: "any contractor generated materials".
- Emterra notice of delays, Sept 10, 2026 (staffing, Labor Day). https://www.flinttownship.org/Documents/Department/Clerk/Public%20Notices/Emterra.pdf (not used on pages)
- Open burning guidelines (Flint Township Fire): "The open burning of stumps, tree cuttings greater than three (3) inches in diameter, leaves, pine needles, or grass clippings is prohibited." Burning only on the property where the material was generated; not between midnight and 6 AM; not when wind exceeds 10 mph or it is raining. https://www.flinttownship.org/departments/fire/burning_permits.php

## Homes, people, business
- U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020-2024 5-year, Flint charter township (through Census Reporter's API, https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B25034,B25035,B25024,B25003&geo_ids=06000US2604929020):
  - Median year structure built: 1970.
  - 1950s 13.9%; 1960s 26.7% (the largest decade); 1970s 20.6%; 1980s 10.7%; 1990s 8.8%; 2000 or later about 10%; before 1950 about 9.5%. So about 61% of homes date from 1950 to 1979.
  - Units: 59.0% single family detached; 5.7% attached; about 24% in buildings of three or more units (10.5% in 10 to 19 unit buildings); 11.2% mobile homes.
  - Tenure: 60.9% owner occupied.
- Charter Township of Flint Economic Development and Marketing Plan (2019): https://cms3.revize.com/revize/flinttownship/Documents/Government/Boards%20and%20Commissions/Economic%20Development/Economic%20Development%20and%20M.pdf
  - Retail along the Linden, Corunna and Miller Road corridors.
  - DDA district (2018) mainly along South Linden, Corunna, Lennon, Miller and Bristol Roads near I-75 and I-69; holds much of the township's commercial and industrial uses.
  - Beecher Road corridor: commercial and office, near McLaren Flint hospital just outside the township.
  - Bristol Road corridor: commercial and industrial; Baker College is the major feature.
  - Large employers named: HAP, Genesee Valley Center, FedEx, UPS, numerous medical facilities.
- Wikipedia (general only): population 31,447 (2020); 23.6 square miles; "mostly an L-shape around the southwest side of the City of Flint"; Bishop International Airport adjacent and occupying part of the township; I-75, I-69 and US 23 meet about a mile from the airport; first schoolhouse (1838) on the bank of Swartz Creek; Genesee Valley Center opened August 1970 at Miller and Linden Roads. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint_Township,_Michigan and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesee_Valley_Center

## Soils (USDA NRCS Soil Data Access, Genesee County survey MI049)
- Queried 2026-11-21, area weighted inside a box over the township's west side (lon -83.82 to -83.745, lat 42.97 to 43.04; approximate, includes some land outside the township):
  - Conover loam, 0 to 4% slopes, 62.6%; Brookston loam, dense substratum, 0 to 1% slopes, 11.4%; Wawasee loam 6.8% (two slope classes); smaller areas of Udorthents and Udipsamments, Metamora sandy loam, Ceresco, Perrin loamy sand, Boyer and Spinks-Oakville sands.
  - Drainage classes: Conover somewhat poorly drained; Brookston poorly drained; Wawasee well drained; Boyer and Spinks well drained.
- What it means on the pages: mostly flat, heavy loam that holds water in spring and after big rains; soft ground and standing water in low spots; beds and grades need to move water away from the house.

## Turf and plant care (MSU Extension)
canr.msu.edu refused automated reads (HTTP 403) on 2026-11-21; these points come from quoted text in search results and are kept general.
- Mowing: no more than one third of the blade per cut; 2.5 to 3.5 inches general range; spring growth fast. https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/mowing-lawn-turf
- Pruning: spring flowering shrubs are best pruned right after they bloom, because they set next year's flower buds the summer before; summer flowering shrubs in late winter or early spring; dead or damaged wood can come out in early spring. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/pruning_spring_flowering_shrubs
- Leaves: mulching leaves into the lawn with a mower showed no harm in MSU research. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/its_not_too_late_to_mulch_fallen_leaves_into_lawns
- Mulch: 2 to 3 inches, kept off trunks. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/mulch_overload
- Salt: spray and soil buildup damage plants; do not pile salty snow on plantings. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/salt_damage_on_landscape_plants
