# Swartz Creek, MI: research notes

City of Swartz Creek, west of Flint, Genesee County. All sources read 2026-11-21. Facts here feed `src/content/cities/swartz-creek.json` and the Swartz Creek town pages. Nothing is linked from the site; rules are paraphrased with "the city's page says" or "check with City Hall".

## Neighborhoods and housing (City of Swartz Creek Master Plan Update, SmithGroup, adopted 2022)
Source: https://cityofswartzcreekmi.gov/images/2022MasterPlanLowRes2.pdf
- Single family housing is about 45% of the city's land use, "concentrated on the west side of town, in and around Winchester Village. Most of these dwellings were built to accommodate industrial workers and their families in the 1950s and 60s." Growing single family areas in Heritage Village and the Miller neighborhood east of downtown. (p. 40)
- Age of housing stock (2019 ACS, p. 43): 172 units 1939 or earlier; 93 in the 1940s; 510 in the 1950s; 371 in the 1960s; 458 in the 1970s; 264 in the 1980s; 334 in the 1990s; 383 in 2000 to 2009; 41 in 2014 or later. 2,802 housing units at the 2020 census.
- Winchester Village (p. 50): "approximately sixty years old ... one of the first uniform housing developments to be completed with sewer, water, paved streets, sidewalks and street trees. The Village accommodates approximately 2,000 residents." Ongoing 20 year street plan work.
- Park, Elms and Atkins Acres: "Most lots are small and developed as single family housing." Parkridge and Otterburn Heights: platted subdivisions "with one or two outlets", built out and stable. (p. 46)
- Heritage Village: a planned condominium community with "alleys, sidewalks, and common areas", only partially developed; an example of traditional neighborhood design. (pp. 45 to 46)
- Markwood: "very low density residential single family, with much woodland. The lots are generally narrow, but large". Winchester Woods has vacant lots that "might be developable and buildable if drainage and other utility improvements are ever pursued". Crapo has "some of the oldest plats in the City". (p. 55)
- Local streets (p. 22): "Most local streets have sidewalks, on-street parking, and right-of-way trees." The city enforces its sidewalk repair and snow clearing ordinances.

## The creek, wetlands and soil (Master Plan, pp. 36 to 37)
- "The most prominent water body in the City is the Swartz Creek. This stream serves primarily as a drain for the City." Its watershed "lies mostly to the south of the City and eventually empties into the Flint River." FEMA 100 year floodplain along it.
- Wetlands of several kinds inside the city (mixed wooded, lowland hardwood of ash, elm, soft maple and cottonwood, lowland conifer, emergent, shrub/scrub).
- Soil: "Because the City of Swartz Creek is integrated with a flood plain, the water table is moderate to high and is associated with poor drainage and wet soil. This means that the soil is not naturally suited for development without the addition of sand or through site mitigation".
- The Holly Township master plan notes Swartz Creek (the stream) runs through the central northern part of Holly Township, so its upper reaches lie south of the city. https://hollytownship.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2024-Adopted-MAster-Plan-FINAL.pdf
- Fall 2026 city newsletter: the trail "from Morrish Road to Fairchild Street ... follows the water and is tree lined."

## Snow rules
- Sidewalks, Sec. 15-21 (city sidewalk notice, 2025): the occupant, or owner of unoccupied premises, keeps the sidewalk clear; snow and ice "must be removed within 48 hours of the time it fell or accumulated"; the city may clear it and the cost becomes a debt and lien on the property; civil infraction. Walks 60 inches wide or less are cleared in their entirety; wider walks need only 60 inches cleared.
  Source: https://cityofswartzcreekmi.gov/images/SIDEWALKNOTICE2025.pdf (also summarized on the 2026 city calendar)
- Winter reminders (Fall 2026 newsletter): "There is no parking on any city street from 2am-6am." "Property owners are responsible for clearing their sidewalks after snow and ice events! Folks leaving town for the winter should make arrangements to have this done." Failure can mean clearing by a contractor at the owner's expense and fines.
  Source: https://cityofswartzcreekmi.gov/images/Newsletter2026Fall.pdf
- Department of Public Services page: DPS "is responsible for snow removal on City streets during winter months. Please remember that driveway snow is not to be plowed into the road. Meter reading devices, fire hydrants and sidewalks should all be kept clear of snow."
  Source: https://www.cityofswartzcreekmi.gov/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=61
- Michigan Vehicle Code MCL 257.677a(4): "A person shall not deposit, or cause to be deposited, snow, ice or slush on any roadway or highway." https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-677A.pdf
- Genesee County Road Commission (county roads in the townships around the city, such as Clayton Township, whose hall has a Swartz Creek address): roads plowed by priority on traffic volume; "Subdivision streets are the final priority." Owners should inspect mailboxes and posts before winter. Rocks, solid timber garden borders and heavy mailbox posts in the right of way are hazards and need a permit.
  Sources: https://gcrc.org/about/frequently-asked-questions ; Clayton Township Hall address, 2011 S. Morrish Road, Swartz Creek, on https://www.scafd.com/

## Yard waste, leaves, grass
- 2026 city calendar: yard waste picked up Tuesday with trash, April through November; paper yard waste bags or a properly marked container; 30 lbs or less; stickers at City Hall; "NO PLASTIC BAGS". Brush up to 2 inches diameter and 4 feet long, tied in bundles no more than 18 inches across. Brush chipping April through October, every other week: cut ends to the road, up to 4 inches diameter and 4 feet long, at the curb by 8 a.m. Monday of the chipping week.
  Source: https://www.cityofswartzcreekmi.gov/2026%20CALANDAR.pdf
- No city leaf vacuum or loose leaf collection found on any city page or document read. Copy does not claim one or deny one; it says leaves go out in paper yard waste bags per the city calendar.
- Fall 2026 newsletter: "Do not dump your leaves into storm drains or ditches!" "All storm drains are connected directly to the river!"
- Weed and grass ordinance (Sec. 20-21, per the 2026 calendar): no grass or rank vegetation over six inches on average.
- Burning rules: not found on the city or Swartz Creek Area Fire Department sites. Left out.

## Turf and plant facts used (MSU Extension, read via web.archive.org copies because canr.msu.edu blocks automated reading)
- Mow at 3.5 inches; raise in fall too. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/raise_your_mower_height_to_35_inches_to_help_grass_withstand_grub_damage
- "Compacted clay soils do not accept water readily and runoff can occur from sloping sites." Soils with more silt, clay or organic matter hold more water. https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/irrigation-practices-to-preserve-water-quality
- Leaf mat over winter smothers turf, slows green-up, may mean more snow mold. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/turfgrass-fall-wrap-up-and-snow-mold
- Mulch two to three inches, no volcano, six inch ring at the trunk. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/mulch_just_do_it
- Salt builds up in beds next to sidewalks and driveways; white pines, roses, rhododendrons and yews are sensitive. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/salt_damage_on_landscape_plants
