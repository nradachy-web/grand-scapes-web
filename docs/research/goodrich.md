# Goodrich: research notes

Covers the Village of Goodrich and the surrounding Atlas Township (Genesee County), east of Grand Blanc. All sources read 2026-11-21 unless noted. Official sources first; Wikipedia only for general geography.

## The village and the township
- Village of Goodrich: incorporated 1957; "the community's character remains closely linked with surrounding Atlas Township". Village office 7338 S. State Road, (810) 636-2570.
  Source: https://villageofgoodrich.com/village-history/ ; https://villageofgoodrich.com/
- Village: 2.26 square miles total, 2.19 land and 0.07 water; 2020 census population 2,022. Located within Atlas Township.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodrich,_Michigan (geography only)
- Atlas Township: 36.0 square miles; 2020 population 8,352. Township hall 7386 S. Gale Road, (810) 636-2548.
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Township,_Michigan ; https://www.atlastownship.org/business-departments/trash-and-recycling/

## Village land, homes and natural features (Village of Goodrich Master Plan, adopted 2022)
Source: https://villageofgoodrich.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/MASTER-PLAN-ADOPTED-AUG-8TH-combined.pdf (scanned; read as page images)
- Natural features: "the Mill Pond, rolling hillsides, wetlands, wildlife habitats, mature vegetation, impressive vistas, Kearsley Creek, and a large amount of open space." (p. 17)
- "The Mill Pond and Kearsley Creek are two natural assets which contribute to the quaint character of the Village." The Goodrich family and others cleared the forest land and dammed Kearsley Creek to create the Mill Pond. (p. 19)
- Wetlands "generally fall within low lying areas, around the Mill Pond, and along creeks and water courses." FEMA 100-year floodplains exist in the village. Steep and rolling slopes noted. (p. 18)
- Soils: "In areas without other natural features constraints (i.e. steep slope, wetlands), the Village of Goodrich is made up of primarily well and moderately suited soils." (p. 18, citing the 2018 USDA Genesee County Soil Survey)
- Housing (2013-2017 ACS): 702 housing units; built before 1950: 140; 1950 to 1979: 98; after 1980: 464. About 88 percent owner occupied. (p. 13)
- Land use: residential acreage 127 acres (1990), 456 (2007), 510 (2020). "development of over 380 acres of vacant and agricultural lands into single family residential neighborhoods". Very low density residential = larger lots in the outlying areas; low and medium density = smaller lots in the central village. (p. 24)
- What it means: a small old core around the downtown and the Mill Pond, and mostly newer neighborhoods (two of every three homes built after 1980) on what was farmland.

## Atlas Township land (Atlas Township 2040 Master Plan, adopted 2025)
Source: https://www.atlastownship.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Atlas-2040-Master-Plan-Adopted.pdf
- Topography "gently rolling to hilly with a few steep or rugged areas"; elevations from 972 ft near Horton and Jossman Roads to 810 ft along Kearsley Creek at the north township line.
- Lakes: Shinanguag Lake (238 acres), Atlas Mill Pond (51), Gale Lake (15), Seeley Lake (13), Cummings Lake (6), Kuhn Lake (2).
- Creeks and drains: Cummings Drain, Thread Creek, Zimmerman Creek, Branch Creek, Kearsley Creek, Hoyle Drain, Hasler Creek, Raddison Drain.
- Soils: about 60 percent well drained; about 20 percent somewhat poorly drained; about 15 percent very poorly drained, especially in the northwest around Kearsley Creek, Cummings Lake and Seeley Lake. Poor drainage "may cause damp basements".
- Woodlands cover about 4,670 acres, 22 percent of the township.
- Single family residential is 37.5 percent of the township (8,083 acres in 2023, up from 7,681 in 2008); "Residential use is generally located along major roads, on section line plats, or on large lots"; large lot subdivisions and site condos "create a rural estate-like atmosphere". Minimum residential lot without public sewer: two acres; plan recommends 3 acres in some districts.
- Roads: "many of which have a gravel surface"; 44.4 miles of gravel roads in the township. Local roads, "including all section and quarter section line roads, are under the jurisdiction of the GCRC", aside from private roads in developments.
- What it means: big lots, long drives off gravel roads, woods along the lot lines, wet low spots near creeks and lakes, slopes.

## Soil (USDA)
- USDA NRCS Soil Data Access point queries, run 2026-11-21 (https://sdmdataaccess.sc.egov.usda.gov):
  - Village (42.9170, -83.5063): Celina-Conover loams, 2 to 6 percent slopes (Celina moderately well drained, Conover somewhat poorly drained).
  - Just south of the village (42.905, -83.51) and a point in Atlas Township (42.94, -83.48): Houghton muck, 0 to 1 percent slopes, very poorly drained.

  Source: https://villageofgoodrich.com/waste-collection/ (calendar image https://villageofgoodrich.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Screenshot-2025-11-26-121801.png)

## Burning
- Atlas Township Fire Department burn permit, valid January 1 to December 31, 2026: check the fire department website for burning conditions before any burning; open burning in daylight only, out by dusk; residential only; wind not over 10 mph; pile no larger than 6 feet across; not within 50 feet of a structure; no burning along roadways; attended by someone 18 or older; allowed materials include dry, seasoned brush and leaves. "Township and Village ordinances mandate these rules."
  Source: https://atlastownshipfire.org/burn-permit%2Fdownloads (Burn Permit 2026 PDF)
- Atlas Township Ordinance 192 (No. 6A): permit from the Fire Chief, no charge; no open fire before sunrise or later than a half hour after sunset.
  Source: https://www.atlastownship.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/192.0-Open-Burning-6A.pdf
- Pages say: burning has a permit and rules; check with the fire department; we haul leaves away instead.

## Sidewalk snow
- Atlas Township Sidewalk Ordinance (Part 200, adopted 2022-06-27): property owners maintain public sidewalks on or adjoining their property; "Sidewalks shall be kept free and clear of ice and snow. Snow shall not be piled in a manner that might obstruct the vision of drivers or that blocks or impairs travel on a sidewalk or road." An HOA or condo association may take on sidewalk maintenance. Does not regulate the existing 10-foot non-motorized pathway.
  Source: https://www.atlastownship.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/200.00-SIDEWALK-ORDINANCE-Adopted-June-27-2022.pdf
- Village of Goodrich code (Municode, Chapter 28 Streets, Sidewalks) could not be opened (bot check). Pages say "check with the village office" for village sidewalks.

## Michigan law on snow in the road
- MCL 257.677a(4): "A person shall not deposit, or cause to be deposited, snow, ice or slush on any roadway or highway."
  Source: https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-257-677a

## Road Commission
- GCRC winter priority ends with county local roads (unpaved) and subdivision streets; "Road crews do not work overtime on these roads or streets." Mailboxes damaged during snow removal are replaced with a standard mailbox and/or post after roads are cleared; owners are encouraged to inspect mailboxes and posts before winter. GCRC grades gravel roads and applies chloride for dust in summer.
  Sources: https://www.gcrc.org/practices ; https://gcrc.org/about/frequently-asked-questions

## General horticulture (MSU Extension)
- One third rule, 2.5 to 3.5 inch mowing height: https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/mowing-lawn-turf
- Leaf mats smother turf, slow green-up, can mean more snow mold: https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/mulch_leaves_into_turf_for_a_smart_lawn
- 2 to 3 inches of mulch; keep it off trunks: https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/mulching_landscape_beds_faqs
- Some pollinators overwinter in stems and leaf litter; mid to late April is likely fine for most cavity-nesting bees: https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/when-is-it-safe-to-clean-up-my-yard-this-spring
