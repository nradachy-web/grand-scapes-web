# Research: Fenton (City of Fenton and Fenton Charter Township)

For `src/content/cities/fenton.json` and the Fenton town pages (lawn-mowing, snow-removal, landscaping, fall-cleanup, flower-beds, spring-cleanup, shrub-trimming, commercial).
All read 2026-11-21 unless a capture date is given. Quotes are exact. Two governments: the City of Fenton (downtown on the Shiawassee River) and Fenton Charter Township (the lakes, including Lake Fenton). fentontownship.org refuses automated readers, so township pages were read from their Wayback Machine captures; the capture date is given.

## Geography

Wikipedia (general geography only).
- "Fenton, Michigan": city area 7.00 square miles; it "lies mostly in Genesee County, with small portions in neighboring Oakland and Livingston Counties"; one of two Michigan municipalities in three counties. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenton,_Michigan
- "Fenton Township, Michigan": 27.5 square miles, of which 3.7 square miles (13.29%) is water. The cities of Fenton and Linden were formed from township land. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenton_Township,_Michigan
- "Lake Fenton, Michigan": the CDP "occupies the northeast corner of Fenton Township"; the lake was formerly Long Lake. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Fenton,_Michigan
- "Shiawassee River": it "flows through Holly, Fenton, Linden, Byron..."; paddlers "do a small portage at Fenton dam, and continue on to Lake Ponemah". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiawassee_River

## Soil

USDA NRCS Soil Data Access (SSURGO, survey area MI049), queried 2026-11-21, 6 by 6 point grid per area.
- City of Fenton (36 points): Wawasee loam (8), Conover loam (7), Boyer loamy sand (7, three slope classes up to 12 to 18 percent), Wasepi sandy loam (4), mucks (Edwards 2, Carlisle and Linwood 1), Fox, Gilford, Marlette, Miami, Sisson, Oakville fine sand.
- Fenton Township lakes area north of the city (36 points): Conover loam (11), Water (7), Carlisle and Linwood mucks (6), Wawasee loam (4), Boyer loamy sand (2), others single.
- Drainage: Boyer "Well drained" (coarse-loamy, sandy); Wawasee "Well drained"; Wasepi "Somewhat poorly drained"; Conover "Somewhat poorly drained"; Carlisle and Linwood "Very poorly drained" mucks.
- Use on the site: a real mix in one town, sandy knolls that dry out next to wet muck near the water. Stated generally.

## City of Fenton: public works, waste, brush

City of Fenton, Department of Public Works. https://www.cityoffenton.org/220/Department-of-Public-Works
- "The work crew maintains over 60 miles of streets within the city."
- "the DPW crew also plows snow from our streets and hauls it from the downtown area during the winter months."
- The department "trims and removes trees, hauls brush for residents".
City of Fenton, Waste Collection and Recycling. https://www.cityoffenton.org/180/Waste-Collection
- "Refuse, Recycling and Yard-Waste pickup is on Wednesday." "Yard Waste is collected on Wednesdays from April through November, starting April 8th, 2026." Hauler: Priority Waste.
- No city leaf vacuum program found on the city site. Not claimed.
City of Fenton, Brush Chipping Program. https://cityoffenton.org/221/Brush-Chipping-Program
- Runs "from May 1st through September 30th"; "limited to one collection per year"; call City Hall "prior to Friday noon for collection on the following Monday".
- "Place brush alongside the roadway with the cut ends facing the street. Maximum material size is 12 inches in diameter with a cut length no longer than 8 feet. Pile size is limited to 4 feet in height, 8 feet in depth and 30 feet in width."
- "We cannot accept: bagged material, lawn clippings, vine/thorn/thistle material, root balls, construction material, landscape timbers, railroad ties..."

## City of Fenton: zoning (parking lots, river floodplain)

City of Fenton Zoning Ordinance, Article 19, Off-Street Parking and Loading (amended January 14, 2008). https://www.cityoffenton.org/DocumentCenter/View/233/Article-19-Parking-and-Loading-PDF
- Sec. 19.07: "All off-street parking and loading facilities required by this Article shall be maintained free of accumulated snow or standing water which prevent full use and occupancy of such facilities, except for temporary periods of no more than five (5) days in the event of heavy rainfall or snowfall."
- Residential: "A minimum three (3) feet wide lawn or landscape strip shall be required between the edge of parking area pavement and all property lines to provide adequate room for drainage, snow storage and privacy screening."
- "No parking shall be permitted in required yards on a regular basis on lawns or other unpaved areas on residential lots."
City of Fenton Zoning Ordinance, Article 24, Environmental Protection. https://cityoffenton.org/DocumentCenter/View/205/Article-24-Environmental-Protection-PDF
- Sec. 24.03 Flood Hazard Area Overlay Zone follows the 100 year flood area in the Flood Insurance Study for the city.
- Sec. 24.03(e): "Dredging and filling and/or dumping or backfilling with any material in any manner is prohibited unless through compensating excavation and shaping of the floodplain, the flow and impoundment capacity of the floodplain will be maintained or improved, and unless all applicable state regulations are met".
- Sec. 24.05 Wellhead Protection Overlay Zone: groundwater "is, or may be in the future, the sole source of the City's drinking water supply".
- City code of ordinances is on Municode, which refuses automated readers; no city sidewalk snow rule is stated on the site.

## Genesee County Road Commission

GCRC Winter Maintenance Policy (PDF). https://www.gcrc.org/forms-documents/forms/policies/240-winter-maintenance-policy/file
- "CLASS 5 (Subdivisions and Gravel Local Roads): ... Blading and plowing shall generally be the only treatment given." Synopsis: "Fourth Priority: Snow covered with plowing and grading. No overtime authorized."
- "Please give 24 hours for Main roads to be plowed and 48 hours for local roads."
- "Works crews usually plow half of a road to one side and then the other. This means snow is pushed into driveways. This is unavoidable."
- "The trucks are normally blading back the gutter area to allow snowmelt to get to sewers and to widen the traveled area."
GCRC FAQ. https://www.gcrc.org/about/frequently-asked-questions
- Salt "does run off into lakes and streams."
- Mailboxes: "Property owners are encouraged to inspect their mailboxes/supports before the winter season each year".
- "A permit is required to install anything within the public right of way."

## MSU Extension (read through Wayback captures; canr.msu.edu blocks automated readers)

- "Reap the benefits of natural shoreline landscapes" (capture 2026-02-15). https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/reap_the_benefits_of_natural_shoreline_landscapes
  "Lawn grasses like Kentucky bluegrass and fine fescue do not have the deep root systems that native shoreline plants naturally provide along the water’s edge. Shallow rooted plants cannot stabilize the shoreline from wave action, increasing the potential for bank erosion." "a buffer strip of native vegetation between turf and the water would be of great benefit to the health of the lake."
- "Go Green" lawn care tip sheet (PDF capture). https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/files/3-20GoGreenTipSheet.pdf
  "Mow at least 3” high"; "Sweep or blow clippings from walks and driveways onto the lawn"; "Never discharge clippings near lakes, rivers, streams or drains".
- "Salt damage on landscape plants" (capture 2025-12-14). https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/salt_damage_on_landscape_plants
  Damage occurs "where large concentrations build up in landscape beds adjacent to sidewalks and driveways." Calcium chloride or calcium magnesium acetate "are safer to use around plants, but are more expensive." Burlap barriers; salt tolerant plantings.
- "Mowing Lawn Turf", Lyman and Rieke (capture 2025-12-12). https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/turf-tips-for-the-homeowner-mowing-lawn-turf
  "not to remove more than one-third of the total leaf surface"; "up to 6 inches of various types of tree leaves were mowed into lawns. No adverse effects were detected".
- Mulch, pruning and box tree moth facts: as sourced in `docs/research/services-landscape.md` (MSU mulching FAQ: "2 to 3 inches of mulch is adequate"; MSU pruning: "Spring flowering shrubs are best pruned right after they are done blooming"; MDARD 2026-09-22: box tree moth confirmed in Genesee County).
- Last mow and spring cleanup facts: as sourced in `docs/research/services-lawn.md`.
