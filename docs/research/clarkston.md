# Clarkston, MI (City of the Village of Clarkston and Independence Township)

Research for the Clarkston hub and the Clarkston service pages. Every fact below was read on 2026-11-21 unless noted. Wikipedia is used only for general geography.

## What Clarkston is
- The City of the Village of Clarkston is a city of 0.44 square miles of land, the smallest city by land area in Michigan, surrounded by Independence Township and run separately since it became a city in 1992. Population 928 at the 2020 census. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarkston,_Michigan
- The village was platted by the Clark brothers in 1840 and named Clarkston in 1842; Nelson Clark's 1839 house still stands on North Main Street. Source: https://www.villageofclarkston.org/community/history.php
- The Village was designated a Michigan State Historic Site in 1976 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1979. The district includes Buffalo, Church, Depot, Holcomb, Main (M-15), Miller, Waldon and Washington streets and Clarkston Road. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarkston,_Michigan
- Independence Township: population 36,686 (2020 census). 2023 verified land use: rural residential 28.79% (largest), single family residential 20.02%, recreation/open space 17.08%, vacant/agricultural 15.51%, estate residential 7.80%. Source: https://www.indtwp.com/residents/township_profile/index.php
- Township history: outside the flat Sashabaw Plains in the southeast, "the rest of the township was a mix of flat plains, rolling hills and numerous lakes, connected by branches of the Clinton River", and the lakes drew summer vacationers, then cottages, then lakeside farms were subdivided. Source: https://www.indtwp.com/residents/township_history/index.php
- The township lists Pine Knob Ski & Snowboard and DTE Music Theatre among local venues (hilly ground). Source: https://www.indtwp.com/
- The Clinton River's main branch rises from wetlands and coldwater tributaries in Independence, Brandon and Springfield townships. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_River
- Deer Lake is the first lake the Clinton River reaches in Independence Township. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer_Lake_(Independence_Township,_Michigan)
- The Historic District map shows Mill Pond, Upper Mill Pond and Parke Lake inside or along the city. Source: HDC brochure, https://www.villageofclarkston.org/Documents/Government/Boards%20Committees%20Commissions/Historic%20District%20Commission/HDC_OnlineBrochure.pdf

## Soils (USDA)
Method: USDA NRCS Soil Data Access (https://sdmdataaccess.sc.egov.usda.gov), map unit at each point of an even grid, 2026-11-21. Rough tally, not acreage.
- City (25 points): Houghton and Adrian mucks 4; Oshtemo-Boyer loamy sands 0 to 6% slopes 4; Urban land-Spinks complex 0 to 8% 4; Urban land-Spinks 8 to 15% 2; Oshtemo-Boyer 6 to 12% 2; Oshtemo-Boyer 12 to 40% 1; Fox sandy loam 2; water 2.
- Independence Township (64 points): Urban land-Spinks 7; Houghton and Adrian mucks 6; water 5; Spinks loamy sand 0 to 6% 5; Oshtemo-Boyer loamy sands 12 to 40% slopes 4; Fox sandy loam 4 plus 3; Marlette sandy loam 4; Marlette loam 18 to 35% slopes 2; others single.
- Component data (Oakland County survey MI125): Oshtemo, Boyer and Spinks are well drained, formed in outwash or glaciofluvial deposits; Fox is well drained loam over sand and gravel; Marlette is well drained, formed in till; Houghton and Adrian are very poorly drained mucks.
- Meaning for the copy: mostly sandy, fast draining ground on hills, with wet muck in the low spots by lakes and wetlands. Slopes in some map units run 12 to 40 percent.

## Leaves, burning, yard waste
- City code 91.32: "Burning of leaves is strictly prohibited by anyone within the city limits, without exception." For burning, yard waste means brush, limbs and tree cuttings only; leaves and grass clippings are not yard waste. Source: Title IX General Regulations, https://www.villageofclarkston.org/Documents/Document%20Center/City%20Documents/Code%20of%20Ordinances/Title%20IX%20General%20Regulations.pdf
- City code 94.30: unlawful to allow leaves to accumulate at or by, or flow into, any street, alley, ditch, creek, lake or pond in the city. Same source.
- Independence Fire Department (serves township and city): since August 8, 2023 no burn permit is needed in the township or the city, but the ordinance still applies. Parcels under 3 acres follow the burn day calendar; over 3 acres may burn any day. Summer hours April 1 to November 30, 8 AM to 8 PM. No burning within 50 feet of a structure, none along roadways, piles no larger than 6 feet across, attended by someone 14 or older. Dry leaves are acceptable in the township; "leaf burning is not allowed in the City of the Village of Clarkston". Source: https://www.indtwp.com/departments/fire/open_burning_burn_permits.php and https://www.indtwp.com/departments/authorized_burn_days/index.php
- Independence Township licenses several private haulers for residential pickup (list current as of 6/17/2025). So curbside yard waste depends on the hauler a household uses. Source: https://www.indtwp.com/Clerk/2025-2026%20Solid%20Waste%20Haulers%20-%20Licensed%2020250617%20for%20Website.pdf
- No official City of Clarkston leaf pickup schedule was found on the city site. Not stated on any page.
- EGLE: where no local ordinance applies, state rules prohibit burning yard debris within 1,400 feet of an incorporated city or village; burning brush or leaves on lake ice is not allowed (ash into water is prohibited). Source: https://www.michigan.gov/egle/faqs/air-quality/burning

## Lawns near lakes
- Independence Township lawn care tips: mow high (3 inches) and leave clippings; sweep or blow clippings back onto the lawn, not the street, because drains and ditches lead to rivers, lakes and beaches; keep a 15 foot buffer along waterfronts (let the grass grow), which also discourages geese. Source: https://www.indtwp.com/departments/public_works/lawn_care_tips.php
- City resources page lists "Making Clarkston a Greener Place" documents, including Lawn Care, Riparian Information, Goose Management and Home Rain Garden Guidelines. Source: https://villageofclarkston.org/community/resources.php

## Historic district rules that touch yard work
- HDC brochure: any exterior change in the district, "including windows, fences, demolition, new construction, and landscape", must be approved by the Historic District Commission before a building permit. "Contact the HDC before: removing large trees, shrubbery, plantings; installing new or replacement fencing; reconstructing areas of masonry walls..." "Go ahead with your project if: trimming or pruning trees, shrubs, plantings." Fence, paving, wall and landscaping work needs a site plan drawing. The HDC meets the second Tuesday of each month; applications go in at least a week ahead; some work can be approved administratively. Source: HDC brochure (above) and https://www.villageofclarkston.org/government/boards_committees_commissions/historic_district_commission/index.php
- City code 96.05: trees over city streets must be kept trimmed so no branch is lower than eight feet above the sidewalk; that is the owner's duty. 96.01: planting a tree in the street strip needs the DPW to designate the spot. Source: Title IX (above).
- The city's Tree Committee inventories city trees with an arborist. Source: https://www.villageofclarkston.org/government/boards_committees_commissions/tree_committee.php

## Snow
- City DPW: responsible for snow removal from city streets and city sidewalks; starts when roads get slippery. No parking on city streets December 1 to March 1, 2 AM to 6 AM; violators towed or ticketed. Source: https://www.villageofclarkston.org/departments/public_works/snow_removal.php
- City code 93.02: owners or occupants next to public sidewalks keep them free of ice and snow and remove it within 24 hours; otherwise the city may do the work and charge the owner. Source: Title IX (above). Copy says "check with the city" because the DPW page and the code overlap.
- Township roads: public roads are owned and maintained by the Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC); private roads are the responsibility of the homeowners along them. Source: https://www.indtwp.com/residents/road_information.php
- RCOC: sets plowing priorities by traffic volume and speed, with hills and curves also a factor; subdivision streets come after everything else; more than 1,300 miles of subdivision streets; salt loses effectiveness below 20 degrees. Source: https://www.rcocweb.org/160/Snow-Plowing
- RCOC mailboxes: lightweight breakaway supports, post no more than 24 inches deep and not set in concrete, wood no larger than 4 inches square, metal pipe no more than 2 inches across; USPS wants the bottom of the box 42 to 48 inches up. Source: https://www.rcocweb.org/158/Mailboxes
- Independence Township Winter Salt Tips (2020): shovel first, use less than four pounds of salt per 1,000 square feet, sweep up leftover salt, most salts stop working well below 15 degrees (use sand), and chloride that reaches lakes cannot be removed. Source: https://www.indtwp.com/document_center/PublicWorks/EnvironmentalNews/Winter%20Salt%20Tips%202020.pdf
- Michigan law, MCL 257.677a(4): "A person shall not deposit, or cause to be deposited, snow, ice or slush on any roadway or highway." Source: https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-257-677a

## Michigan horticulture (MSU Extension; site blocks automated reading, so these were read from MSU page excerpts in search results on 2026-11-21)
- Do not remove more than one third of the leaf blade in a mowing; mow at about 3 inches or higher. https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/resources/pdfs/mowing_lawn_turf_(e0013).pdf
- Sandy soil holds less water than loam, so lawns on it need water more often. https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/smart_watering_for_lawns_dont_let_the_lawn_squeeze_you_dry
- Mulch 2 to 3 inches deep; keep it off trunks (a mulch free ring). https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/mulching_landscape_beds_faqs
- Leaves can be mowed into the lawn; more than 3 to 4 inches of leaves on the turf is too much. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/its_not_too_late_to_mulch_fallen_leaves_into_lawns
- Spring flowering shrubs (forsythia, lilac) are pruned right after they bloom; summer flowering shrubs in late winter or early spring. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/pruning_spring_flowering_shrubs
- Oaks: do not prune in the high risk period, April 15 to July 15 (oak wilt). https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/stop_pruning_oak_trees_now_to_avoid_oak_wilt_1
- Salt injury is worst on the side facing the road; do not shovel salty snow onto beds; white pine, roses, yews and boxwood are sensitive. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/salt_damage_on_landscape_plants
- Working or walking on wet spring soil compacts it; wait until a squeezed ball crumbles. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/soil_compaction_and_what_to_do_about_it
