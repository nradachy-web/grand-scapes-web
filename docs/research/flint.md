Read 2026-11-21 unless noted. Every fact used on the Flint pages traces to a line here. Where a source could not be loaded, it says so.

## Government and roads
- The City of Flint plows and salts streets inside city limits. Genesee County Road Commission handles highways and interstates (I-75, I-69, I-475). https://www.cityofflint.com/street-maintenance/
- City Street Maintenance maintains 152.26 miles of major streets and 353.89 miles of local streets. Same page.
- Streets under Genesee County Road Commission jurisdiction inside Flint: all of Dort Highway (Carpenter Rd to Hemphill Rd), Court Street (Chavez Dr to Corunna), all of Corunna Road, Ballenger Highway (Sunset Dr to Flushing Rd). Same page (pothole form note).
- Snow plan wording: "safe roads, at safe speeds" not "bare roads"; drivers should still expect snow on the roadway during a storm. City split into six areas plus downtown as a seventh; all six areas get crews at once when over four inches falls in 24 hours. Same page, and the plan PDF below.
- Snow and Ice Control Plan, January 2025 (FY25): https://www.cityofflint.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Snow-Ice-Control-Plan-FY25-1.pdf
  - Priorities: 1 major routes; 2 dangerous hills, curves and intersections, schools and hospitals; 3 Act 51 major roadways; 4 local roads.
  - Local (Priority 4) roads are worked after Priorities 1 to 3 and at the end of the event. For a 4 to 8 inch event, the plan estimates about 72 hours to complete all routes; 8 to 12 inches about 96 hours; over 12 inches about 120 hours. Timelines reset if a new storm starts.
  - Downtown is treated separately, plowed overnight or early morning before parked cars and heavier traffic.
  - For events over 4 inches the city plans to issue no parking advisories to clear neighborhood streets of parked cars.
  - Most resources run 4:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m.; reduced coverage in the evenings.
  - Street Maintenance phone (810) 766-7343.
- Snow emergencies: the city declares them through Alert Flint; residents asked not to park on the street; consequences of parking on public streets during a snow emergency may include ticketing and removal at the owner's expense. https://www.cityofflint.com/winter-storm-warning-please-remove-vehicles-from-city-streets/ (March 3, 2023). During the February 2021 snow emergency "parking on city streets is prohibited". https://www.cityofflint.com/flint-crews-continue-working-hard-to-clean-streets-after-large-snowfall/ (Feb 16, 2021). Alert Flint: https://www.cityofflint.com/alertflint/
- Neighborhood roads are cleared after priority roads are passable. https://www.cityofflint.com/crews-salting-and-plowing-during-winter-weather-advisory/ (Jan 25, 2023)
- Sidewalks and snow: "Sidewalks should be cleared by the property owner. Clearing of sidewalks by property owners is not only required by ordinance, but will greatly enhance the safety of those who need to use them such as school children and mail carriers. When clearing sidewalks, driveways and parking lots, residents and businesses are reminded to not deposit snow into the streets as this hampers plowing efforts." Fire hydrants "should also be cleared by property owners". https://www.cityofflint.com/city-of-flint-snow-removal-efforts-have-cleared-most-major-roads-work-will-soon-continue-into-residential-areas/ (Feb 2, 2015; old, so the pages say "the city has said" and "check with the city").
- The ordinance text itself (Flint Code ch. 42, Streets and Sidewalks, reported as sec. 42-46) is on codelibrary.amlegal.com, which refused automated reads (HTTP 403) on 2026-11-21. No deadline or penalty is stated on the pages because it could not be read.
- Sidewalk maintenance is the homeowner's responsibility per city ordinance; 50/50 Sidewalk Replacement Program exists. https://www.cityofflint.com/street-maintenance/
- Right of way: "Contractors and residents performing work in the right-of-way must obtain a permit. Permits are required when excavating, breaking pavement, sidewalks or performing other activities within the right-of-way." Permits through Building and Safety Inspections at City Hall. Same page.
- Street trees: dead or hazardous trees and fallen limbs in the public right of way go to the Street Maintenance Division, which assesses the tree. Same page.

## State law (applies in Flint)
- MCL 257.677a(4): "A person shall not deposit, or cause to be deposited, snow, ice or slush on any roadway or highway." (3) bars depositing snow onto or across a roadway or its shoulder in a way that obstructs a driver's safety vision. Governments and their employees are not "persons" under (1)(a). https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-257-677a

## Leaves and yard waste
- Hauler: Priority Waste. Trash, recycling and compost picked up the same day, Monday to Friday by area. Compost (yard waste) pickup period April 1 through the week containing November 30. Up to 20 bags; bags and cans 40 gallons or smaller and under 50 lbs. https://www.cityofflint.com/sanitation/
- 2026 leaf notice (Nov 10, 2026): city Street Maintenance sweeps leaves that have naturally fallen onto city streets. Yard leaves "must be bagged and placed at the curb" on the regular service day. "Leaves should never be raked or blown into the street"; they clog storm drains and can cause street flooding; residents who rake leaves into the street may receive a citation. Bagged leaves collected through November 30. Residents may also mulch leaves into lawns or compost them. Residents asked to move vehicles when crews work their street. https://www.cityofflint.com/city-begins-annual-leaf-cleanup-flint-residents-asked-to-bag-yard-waste/
- Free yard waste bags through Nov 30, 2026, up to four per household per month, at City Hall, Mays Senior and Community Center, McKinley Community Center. https://www.cityofflint.com/free-yard-waste-bags-available-to-flint-residents-through-november-30/ (Sep 9, 2026)
- Tall grass and open burning rules: in the city code (American Legal), not readable on 2026-11-21. Not used.

## Homes and lots
- U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020-2024 5-year, Flint city (read through Census Reporter's API, https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B25034,B25035,B25024,B25003&geo_ids=06000US2604929000):
  - Median year structure built: 1954.
  - Built 1939 or earlier 25.5%; 1940s 12.7%; 1950s 31.4%; 1960s 15.2%; 1970s 8.9%; 1980 or later about 6%. So roughly 70% of homes were built before 1960.
  - Units: 80.6% single family detached.
  - Tenure: 53.8% owner occupied, 46.2% renter occupied.
- Civic Park Historic District: land laid out by Boston landscape architect William Pitkin; GM's Modern Housing Corporation began building in April 1919; 950 homes built by January 1920; National Register 1979. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Park_Historic_District (general history only)
- Imagine Flint master plan summary (adopted 2013): "Traditional Neighborhoods" are where most people live, primarily detached single family homes, supported by schools, community centers, religious institutions and parks. Greenways along the Flint River, Swartz Creek, Gilkey Creek and Kearsley Creek; Thread Lake, Kearsley Reservoir, Flint Park Lake. https://www.cityofflint.com/wp-content/uploads/Flint%20Master%20Plan%20Summary.pdf

## Geography (Wikipedia, general only)
- Flint lies along the Flint River. 34.06 square miles. Terrain low and rolling on the south and east sides, flatter to the northwest. 2020 population 81,252. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint,_Michigan
- Neighborhoods named there: Carriage Town (well preserved Victorian homes), Grand Traverse Street District, Central Park (seven cul-de-sacs), Fairfield Village, Civic Park, East Village and the College Cultural neighborhood, Mott Park, Woodcroft Estates. Institutions: University of Michigan-Flint, Kettering University, Mott Community College, Flint Cultural Center, Hurley Medical Center, McLaren. Same page.
- Not used: snowfall averages (Wikipedia and NWS pages disagree on the normal; left out).

## Soils (USDA NRCS Soil Data Access, Genesee County survey MI049, survey version 8/26/2026)
- Queried 2026-11-21 through https://sdmdataaccess.sc.egov.usda.gov/Tabular/post.rest, area weighted inside a box over central Flint (lon -83.74 to -83.64, lat 42.98 to 43.07; the box is approximate, not the city line):
  - Urban land-Crosier complex, 0 to 2% slopes, 37.8%; Urban land-Crosier-Williamstown complex, 2 to 6%, 19.4%; Conover loam 13.5% (two map units); Urban land-Udorthents complex 8.7%; smaller areas of Urban land-Williamstown, Urban land-Boyer, Urban land-Spinks, Spinks-Oakville loamy sands, Ceresco, and Sloan (frequently flooded) along the river.
  - Component drainage classes from the same database: Crosier somewhat poorly drained (fine-loamy); Conover somewhat poorly drained (fine-loamy); Williamstown moderately well drained; Boyer and Spinks well drained, sandy or coarse loamy; Udorthents are disturbed, cut and fill soils.
- What it means on the pages: much of the city is mapped as "urban land" mixed with heavy, slow draining loam, and a lot of yard soil has been moved or filled over a century of building. Sandier soils show up in pockets near the river and dry out faster.

## Turf and plant care (MSU Extension)
canr.msu.edu refused automated reads (HTTP 403) on 2026-11-21; these points come from the quoted text in search results for these pages, and are kept general on the site.
- Mowing: remove no more than one third of the leaf blade per cut; a general height range of 2.5 to 3.5 inches; a height near 3 inches shades the soil; spring growth can need mowing every four to five days. https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/mowing-lawn-turf and https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/resources/pdfs/mowing_lawn_turf_(e0013).pdf and https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/how_to_care_for_rapidly_growing_lawns
- Leaves: MSU research found no harm from mulching tree leaves into lawns with a mower; heavy layers take more passes. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/its_not_too_late_to_mulch_fallen_leaves_into_lawns
- Mulch: 2 to 3 inches is adequate, more is not better; keep mulch off trunks (no volcano mulch). https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/mulch_overload and https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/nightmare_results_for_appliers_of_volcano_mulch
- Salt: sodium chloride damages plants through spray and through salt built up in soil; avoid piling salty snow on plantings; salt-killed lawn areas may need reseeding in spring. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/salt_damage_on_landscape_plants
