# Burton, MI: research notes

Read 2026-11-21. The live burtonmi.gov site sits behind a bot check that blocks most automated reads, so several city pages were read from their Internet Archive copies (date of the copy given). Facts marked "old" are kept for context only and are not printed as current rules.

## The place
- City of Burton, Genesee County. Organized as Burton Township in 1856; residents voted to incorporate on May 16, 1972 and the city formed July 1, 1972. Population 29,715 (2020 census). Area about 23.4 square miles. Described as a suburb of Flint and the second largest city in Genesee County.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton,_Michigan (read via the Wikipedia API, 2026-11-21)
- The city says Burton "changed from an agricultural community to a thriving suburban community". City Hall, police, DPW and the Veterans Memorial sit on South Center Road at Manor Drive, between East Bristol Road and Maple Avenue. Parks: Kelly Lake Park (40 acres, lake, trails), Settlement Park ("a passive linear park along Thread Creak", sic), For-Mar Nature Preserve and Arboretum (380 acres). Three school districts (Bendle, Bentley, Atherton) lie entirely inside the city.
  Source: https://burtonmi.gov/residents/about_our_city/index.php (Internet Archive copy of 2026-05-02)
- Early settlers (the Athertons) "settled on the Thread Creek in 1835". Neighborhoods named on Wikipedia: Belsay (Belsay Road at the rail line, north of I-69 and Court Street, south of Davison Road) and Lapeer Heights (Belsay and Roberta Roads).
  Source: Wikipedia, as above.
- Kearsley Creek: no source found tying it to Burton. Left out.

## Soils (USDA NRCS Soil Data Access, Genesee County survey MI049)
Point samples at 12 spots confirmed inside Burton by the Census geocoder:
- Conover loam, 0 to 4 percent slopes (somewhat poorly drained, hydrologic group C/D, formed in till): 7 of 12 points.
- Urban land-Crosier complex and Urban land-Crosier-Williamstown complex (Urban land about 45% of the unit, Crosier somewhat poorly drained): on the west side toward Flint.
- Sebewa loam (poorly drained, outwash in drainageways), Brookston loam (poorly drained, till in depressions), Kibbie fine sandy loam (somewhat poorly drained), Celina-Conover loams, Metea loamy sand: one point each.
  Source: https://SDMDataAccess.sc.egov.usda.gov/Tabular/post.rest (queries by point, read 2026-11-21); place check via https://geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder/ (2026-11-21)
- What it means: most Burton lawns sit on loam that drains slowly. Low spots stay wet. Ground mapped as "Urban land" has been cut, filled or built over, so a bed there may start on fill.

## Streets and snow
- Road Division: services 71.52 miles of major streets and 106.43 miles of local streets. Duties include plowing public streets, grading gravel streets, maintaining the storm sewer and ditching projects.
  Source: https://www.burtonmi.gov/departments/department_of_public_works/road_division.php (Internet Archive copy of 2026-07-15)
- Snow and Ice Control Plan: "safe roads at safe speeds", not "bare roads". Priority 1: major roads, dangerous hills, curves and intersections. Priority 2: local streets. The city is divided into 7 areas. A 4 to 8 inch event "will normally take approximately 72 hours to complete all routes"; 8 to 12 inches about 96 hours; over 12 inches about 120 hours. "It may be necessary for the City of Burton to issue no parking advisories during snow emergencies in order to clear neighborhood streets of parked cars", announced by press release and on the city website. Salt is the main material; sand treated with chloride is used at intersections and on icy gravel roads.
  Source: https://www.burtonmi.gov/departments/department_of_public_works/snow_and_ice_control_plan.php (Internet Archive copy of 2025-04-02)
- Road complaints page: "remove the snow within one day on all major streets, and within 2-3 on all locals during a normal snow event". Do not park on the shoulder during a storm; the car "blocks the plow from getting close to the edge of the street, and could be towed." Major roads must be cleared before local roads.
- Sidewalks: "It shall be unlawful for any owner or occupant of any premises within the jurisdictional boundaries of the city to fail to remove any snow or ice which may accumulate on any sidewalk adjacent to the premises within 24 hours after the cessation of any storm and to keep that sidewalk free from obstructions. Violation of this section shall be a municipal civil infraction." Complaints about a business or home go to DPW, 810-742-9230.
- "There are still several gravel roads located in the City of Burton." Graded in spring and fall.
  Source: https://www.burtonmi.gov/departments/department_of_public_works/complaintsRD.php (Internet Archive copy of 2025-04-03)
- Code of Ordinances, Chapter 90 Streets and Sidewalks, lists 90.080 "Snow and ice; removal required" (section text not reachable; wording above is from the city's page). Chapter 71 lists 71.03 "Obstruction of mailbox prohibited".
  Source: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/burtonmi/latest/burton_mi/0-0-0-1523 and 0-0-0-1499 (Internet Archive copies of 2022)
- Drainage: the Road Department maintains the city storm sewer; the Genesee County Drain Commission maintains some storm sewer in the city too. The Road Department keeps a list of properties needing ditch repair.
  Source: https://burtonmi.gov/departments/department_of_public_works/drainage_concerns.php (Internet Archive copy of 2025-04-09)
- Michigan law, MCL 257.677a(4): "A person shall not deposit, or cause to be deposited, snow, ice or slush on any roadway or highway." Civil infraction.
  Source: https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-257-677a (read live 2026-11-21)

## Yard waste, dumping, burning
- "The City of Burton uses EMTERRA Environmental for curbside trash pick-up, recycling, and yard waste removal." New residents call City Hall, 810-743-1500, for their day; the page links an "Emterra Rules" document (not readable to us).
  Source: https://burtonmi.gov/residents/waste_collection.php (read live 2026-11-21)
- Old (2019 resident packet, when Waste Management was the hauler): yard waste first full week of April through last full week of November; brown paper bags or a stickered can of 32 gallons or less; brush in 4 foot bundles. Not printed as current.
  Source: https://www.burtonmi.gov/RESIDENT%20PACKET%202019%2020190227.pdf (Internet Archive copy of 2019-10-29)
- Dump permits: "Each Burton household is entitled to one (1) FREE dump permit per month", a pickup bed under 1,000 lbs; yard waste is an accepted material; GFL Environmental (formerly Brent Run Landfill), Montrose.
- Burning: "If you wish to burn, a permit is REQUIRED. The Fire Department issues burning permits for the city."
  Source: https://burtonmi.gov/government/clerk_s_office/permits.php (Internet Archive copy of 2026-11-03)
- Fire department page: "Open Burning Status: PERMITTED with a VALID Permit."
  Source: https://burtonmi.gov/government/fire_department/index.php (Internet Archive copy of 2026-08-14)
- Code 92.02 defines OPEN BURNING FIRES as a fire "not larger than 4 feet by 5 feet, and primarily intended as a method to dispose of brush, tree limbs or garden debris."
  Source: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/burtonmi/latest/burton_mi/0-0-0-1781 (Internet Archive copy, 2022)
- Old (2019 packet): "BURNING PERMITTED BRUSH, TREE LIMBS, AND GARDEN DEBRIS ONLY. ABSOLUTELY NO LEAVES, GRASS, OR BUILDING MATERIALS." The current Open Burning rules PDF could not be read. Pages say: check with the fire department.

## Tall grass and nuisances
- Code Enforcement enforces Chapter 157 (zoning), Chapter 91 (Health and Sanitation; Nuisances) and 133.04. Works "on a complaint basis"; a valid complaint gets "a ten-day notice"; if the violation remains, "the property owner could receive a ticket, or the property could be cleaned up by the city with all costs being charged to the property owner." A second valid complaint within twelve months skips the notice.
  Source: https://burtonmi.gov/departments/department_of_public_works/code_enforcement_division.php (Internet Archive copy of 2026-08-21)
- Chapter 91.01 is titled "Weeds, grasses, and brush; removal; costs". Height limit not read: left out.
  Source: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/burtonmi/latest/burton_mi/0-0-0-1743 (Internet Archive copy, 2022)

## Horticulture (MSU Extension), used on Burton pages
- Mowing: current standards 2 to 3.75 inches; do not remove more than one third of the leaf surface; may mean mowing every four to five days in fast spring growth; return clippings. https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/resources/pdfs/mowing_lawn_turf_(e0013).pdf (Internet Archive copy of 2025-11-11)
- Turning a mower on wet or saturated soil can compact it; saturated soil starves roots of oxygen. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/how_to_care_for_rapidly_growing_lawns (read 2026-11-21)
- Leaves: mow them in at the highest deck setting when dewy, not soaking; do not let them pile past 3 to 4 inches; for the last cut lower the deck no more than one notch (about half an inch). https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/final_fall_turf_chores (read 2026-11-21); mulching mowers can take up to 6 inches of leaves in a pass: https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/its_not_too_late_to_mulch_fallen_leaves_into_lawns (read 2026-11-21)
- Mulch: 2 to 3 inches is adequate; a 1 inch top dressing over last year's mulch; never pile it at the base of a woody plant ("mulch volcano"); lawn clippings are a poor mulch. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/mulching_landscape_beds_faqs (Internet Archive copy of 2026-05-13)
- Salt: damage comes from spray and from salt that builds up in beds next to walks and drives; white pines, roses, rhododendrons and yews are sensitive; spring rain flushes it; calcium based de-icers are safer near plants. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/salt_damage_on_landscape_plants (Internet Archive copy of 2026-04-18)
- Pruning: lilac, forsythia, spirea, weigela and viburnum set next year's buds the summer before; prune them right after bloom; dead wood can come out in early spring. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/pruning_spring_flowering_shrubs (Internet Archive copy of 2026-01-24)
