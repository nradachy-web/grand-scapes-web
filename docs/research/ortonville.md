# Ortonville, MI (Village of Ortonville and Brandon Township)

Research for the Ortonville hub and the Ortonville service pages. Every fact below was read on 2026-11-21 unless noted. Wikipedia is used only for general geography.

## What Ortonville is
- Village founded by Amos Orton, who came in 1839 and found Kearsley Creek in a heavily wooded area with springs and lakes; he dammed the creek for a gristmill, and the 1856 mill building is now the historical society museum. The village calls itself the commercial center and "downtown" of Brandon Township. Source: https://www.ortonvillevillage.com/community/history.php and https://www.ortonvillevillage.com/community/community_index.php
- The village is about one square mile (0.98 square miles, all land); platted 1866, incorporated 1902; 574 housing units at the 2010 census. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortonville,_Michigan
- The village has its own DPW (DPW manager listed on the staff page). Source: https://www.ortonvillevillage.com/government/village_staff.php
- The village publishes "Yard of the Month Guidelines" for its Yard of the Month award. Source: https://www.ortonvillevillage.com/community/resources.php
- Brandon Township: 35.98 square miles, population 15,384 (2020 census), created 1837. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Township,_Michigan

## Brandon Township Master Plan (adopted 2022)
Source: https://brandontownship.us/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/FINAL-MP-8-31-2022.pdf
- People settle in the township for its "rural atmosphere, characterized in part by rolling hills, woodlands, wetlands, and lakes."
- Median year built of homes: 1984.
- Parcels: under 8,000 sq ft 3,081; 8,000 to 13,999 sq ft 2,014; 14,000 sq ft to 1 acre 2,257; 1 to 2.5 acres 2,081; 2.51 to 5 acres 1,159; 5.01 to 10 acres 574; over 10 acres 306, which cover the most land (35.6%).
- Natural features: tree rows and woodlands 2,315 acres; forested wetland 1,472; scrub-shrub wetland 1,121; emergent wetland 485; water 569.
- Kearsley Creek is a cold water, MDNR managed trout stream whose headwaters are in the township; its subwatershed covers 64% of the township (the western half). Its main impairment is sediment, blamed on streambank erosion, removal of streamside plants, construction sites, gravel roads, roadside ditches and similar.
- Paint Creek's headwaters are on the east side; it is managed as a cold water trout stream from Lake Orion down.
- Lakes on the plan's maps include Lake Louise, Bald Eagle, Perry, Seymour, Huff, Tully, Green, Hummer, Wilson, Cedar and others.
- 134 miles of road in the township. MDOT maintains M-15; the Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC) has all other roads.

## Soils (USDA)
Method: USDA NRCS Soil Data Access (https://sdmdataaccess.sc.egov.usda.gov), map unit at each point of an even grid, 2026-11-21. Rough tally, not acreage.
- Village (25 points): Oshtemo-Boyer loamy sands 0 to 6% slopes 5, 6 to 12% 4; Houghton and Adrian mucks 3; Spinks loamy sand 6 to 12% 3; Urban land-Spinks 3; Sloan silt loam, frequently flooded 2; Granby loamy sand 1.
- Brandon Township (64 points): Houghton and Adrian mucks 9; Riddles sandy loam 6 plus 1; Oshtemo-Boyer loamy sands 6 and 5; Marlette sandy loam 6 to 12% slopes 5, 1 to 6% 4; Capac sandy loam 4; Spinks loamy sand 3 plus 2; others single.
- Component data (Oakland County survey MI125): Oshtemo, Boyer and Spinks are well drained, formed in outwash; Riddles and Marlette are well drained, formed in till; Capac is somewhat poorly drained; Houghton and Adrian are very poorly drained mucks; Sloan (creek bottoms) floods frequently.
- Meaning for the copy: sandy and gravelly rises that drain fast, loamy till on the farm ground, and a lot of wet muck in the low spots.

## Burning, leaves, yard waste
- Brandon Fire Department: open burning by permit only, renewed every January 1, online for residential property. Dry leaves, brush and cut dry grass may be burned; wet leaves and wet grass may not. Parcels under 3 acres follow the burn calendar; 3 acres or more may burn any day. No burning within 50 feet of a structure, none in the road right of way, none in wind or drought; attended by someone 18 or older; pile no larger than 6 feet across. The rules mention both township and village ordinances. Residents of the two mobile home communities cannot get permits. Source: https://brandontownship.us/2020/10/15/burning-permits/
- Clean-up days for township residents, June 6 and August 29, 2026, by appointment, at the Community Park lot on North Hadley Road. Source: https://brandontownship.us/2020/12/28/clean-up-and-recycling/
- EGLE: where no local ordinance applies, state rules prohibit burning yard debris within 1,400 feet of an incorporated city or village; burning on lake ice is not allowed. Source: https://www.michigan.gov/egle/faqs/air-quality/burning
- No township or village curbside leaf pickup was found on an official page. Not stated on any page.

## Trees and plants
- Brandon Township tree page: oak wilt infection rates are increasing; do not prune oaks during the warmer months. Also points owners of dying ash trees to emerald ash borer information. Source: https://brandontownship.us/2020/12/28/trees/
- Phragmites are prevalent in the township; Asian bittersweet is also found there. Source: https://brandontownship.us/2020/02/28/do-you-have-invasive-plants-in-your-yard/

## Snow and roads
- RCOC sets plowing priority by traffic volume and speed, with hills and curves a factor; subdivision streets come last. Salt is not effective on gravel roads, so sand is used there; salt loses effectiveness below 20 degrees. Source: https://www.rcocweb.org/160/Snow-Plowing
- RCOC has about 755 miles of gravel roads in Oakland County; after a storm, primary gravel roads come first, then lighter roads such as subdivision roads; chloride dust control is only on some roads. Source: https://www.rcocweb.org/339/Gravel-Road-Dust-Control-and-Chloride-Sc
- RCOC mailboxes: breakaway support, post no deeper than 24 inches and not set in concrete; USPS wants the bottom of the box 42 to 48 inches up. Source: https://www.rcocweb.org/158/Mailboxes
- MCL 257.677a(4): "A person shall not deposit, or cause to be deposited, snow, ice or slush on any roadway or highway." Source: https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-257-677a
- Village sidewalk snow rules: the village ordinance file sits behind a bot check; not read, so the copy says "check with the village office".

## Michigan horticulture (MSU Extension; read from MSU page excerpts in search results on 2026-11-21, the site blocks automated reading)
- One third rule; mow around 3 inches or higher. https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/resources/pdfs/mowing_lawn_turf_(e0013).pdf
- Sandy soil holds less water, so it needs water more often. https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/smart_watering_for_lawns_dont_let_the_lawn_squeeze_you_dry
- Leaves can be mowed into the lawn; more than 3 to 4 inches is too much. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/its_not_too_late_to_mulch_fallen_leaves_into_lawns
- Mulch 2 to 3 inches, kept off trunks. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/mulching_landscape_beds_faqs
- Oaks: no pruning April 15 to July 15. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/stop_pruning_oak_trees_now_to_avoid_oak_wilt_1
- Salt injury worst on the road side; do not pile salty snow on beds. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/salt_damage_on_landscape_plants
