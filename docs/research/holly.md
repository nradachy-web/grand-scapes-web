# Holly, MI: research notes

Village of Holly and Holly Township, north Oakland County. All sources read 2026-11-21 unless noted. Facts here feed `src/content/cities/holly.json` and the Holly town pages. Nothing is linked from the site; rules are paraphrased with "check with the village / township".

## Who governs what
- Holly Township was organized in 1838; the Village of Holly sits inside the township. The area outside the village limits is "commonly referred to as the outer Township". The township provides village residents assessing, elections, gravel road chloride, and fall and spring cleanup services; the village has its own public works, fire and police.
  Source: https://www.hollytownship.gov/history
- A Holly mailing address does not mean the village. The village police FAQ: the department's service area is the village boundaries only; "If you live in Holly or Rose Township you must call the Michigan State Police."
  Source: https://hollyvillage.org/index.php/government/public-safety/f-a-q
- Village population 5,997 (2020 census); about 3 sq mi, about 9% of it water. General geography only.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly,_Michigan

## Roads and plowing
- Village DPW: "provides snow removal, shoulder maintenance, pothole patching, sidewalk and curb repair and signage services for more than 7 miles of major trunk-line and 15 miles of village streets, including all allies [alleys] and dirt roads." Also maintains the Millpond Dam.
  Source: https://hollyvillage.org/index.php/government/public-works
- Outer township roads are the Road Commission for Oakland County's. Township master plan: "According to the Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC), there are roughly 23 miles of primary road in Holly Township and 38 miles of local roads. A majority of road surfaces are gravel."
  Source: Holly Township Master Plan, adopted 2024-08-21, p. 61. https://hollytownship.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2024-Adopted-MAster-Plan-FINAL.pdf (read via web.archive.org copy)
- RCOC snow plowing page: priorities set by traffic volume and speed; subdivision streets are at the bottom and are "done after everything else is taken care of". "Salt is not effective on gravel roads, so it's used primarily on paved roads, and sand on the gravel roads." Salt loses effectiveness below 20 degrees.
  Source: https://www.rcocweb.org/160/Snow-Plowing
- RCOC "Plowing Subdivisions in Oakland County" brochure (updated 10/26): after a major storm (six or more inches in less than 24 hours) township subdivision streets may not be plowed for several days. RCOC trucks "cannot plow the ends of driveways or around mailboxes" and "will often push snow into driveways or in front of mailboxes". Asks residents to keep vehicles off streets during and after snowfall, not to put rocks at the road edge (plows push them onto the lawn), not to put sprinkler heads close to the road, and to check that the mailbox post is secure before winter.
  Source: https://www.rcocweb.org/DocumentCenter/View/8916/Plowing-Subdivisions-in-Oakland-County-Townships
- RCOC winter brochure (updated 12/25): at 10 degrees or lower salt does virtually nothing; sand is used on gravel roads where there are no storm sewers.
  Source: https://www.rcocweb.org/DocumentCenter/View/8935/Winter-Road-Maintenance-

## Snow rules
- Village snow ordinance No. 461 (2020), amending Sec. 72.01: no parking on village streets between 3:00 a.m. and 7:00 a.m. from December 1 through March 30; parking between sidewalk and street allowed 5 p.m. to 7 a.m. in that period. Published in the village bulletin of 2021-01-06. Possibly amended since: copy says "check with the village".
  Source: https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MIOEVIHOLLY/bulletins/2b46f6f
- Village sidewalk clearing deadline: NOT confirmed. The village code on amlegal (codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/holly) was blocked to automated reading; a search snippet shows a rule against causing "unnatural or artificial accumulation of snow or ice on a sidewalk" (Ord. 346, 2001), but no clearing deadline was read. Left out of the copy.
- Michigan Vehicle Code MCL 257.677a(4): "A person shall not deposit, or cause to be deposited, snow, ice or slush on any roadway or highway." (2) and (3) bar pushing snow onto or across a road or shoulder in a way that blocks a driver's view. Civil infraction.
  Source: https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-677A.pdf

## Leaves, yard waste and burning
- Village: Priority Waste collects for village residents. Compost collection: grass clippings, leaves, shrub and brush trimmings (under 2 inches diameter, bundled and tied, no longer than 4 feet). Weekly collection April 1 to November 30. Paper bags or clearly marked containers, max 35 gallons, under 50 pounds. Root balls, dirt, rocks, oversized branches and plastics not accepted. Brochure dated for July 2024 start.
  Sources: https://hollyvillage.org/index.php/government/public-works/waste-collection and https://hollyvillage.org/images/VillageofHollyPriorityWasteInfo.pdf
- Township: "The Township of Holly does not provide garbage collection services to its residents." No township recycling. Fall Clean Up Day (October 10, 2026, 4092 Grange Hall Rd): "NO ROADSIDE PICKUP ... NO STUMPS, NO LEAVES".
  Source: https://www.hollytownship.gov/waste
- Village burning (Holly Fire Department burn permit page): open burning by permit only, issued by the village; no burning within 50 feet of a structure. Items that cannot be burned include "BRUSH" and "LEAVES".
  Source: https://www.hollyfiredepartment.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=9&catid=10&Itemid=154
- Township burning: "All open burning in Holly Township, by ordinance, requires a permit from NOCFA", with a call to the North Oakland County Fire Authority for a permit for the day.
  Source: archived township fire page, https://web.archive.org/web/20260613150431/https://hollytownship.org/about-your-government/fire-department/

## Land, water and soil
- Township master plan (2024), Water Resources: "More than 35 lakes of varying size are scattered extensively throughout the Township, occupying 6% of total land area. Large areas of wetlands surround the many water bodies as well. The Shiawassee River traverses through the southern portion of the Township, and Swartz Creek is located in the central northern portion."
- Bush Lake system: "A large portion of the area to the east of Bush Lake is built-out"; Holly Township Beach Park and Lakeside Park at its southwest corner. Lake Copneconic in the northwest corner. Holly State Recreation system along most of the eastern boundary, with Gravel, Strawberry, Holdridge and Horton Lakes and wetland along Swartz Creek; the central and southern portions near Spring Lake and Little Crotched Lake have been developed.
- Holly State Recreation Area: 7,817 acres shared with Groveland Township. Seven Lakes State Park: 1,444 acres, 230 acres of water, opened 1977, campground on Sand Lake.
- Geology: the township is in southeast Michigan's "hilly zone"; till plain, moraine and some outwash plain; "deposits mainly of sand and gravel".
- Soils: four broad types. Marlette-Capac-Houghton (loamy and mucky, well drained to very poorly drained, till plains, moraines and bogs) is most common; Oshtemo-Spinks-Houghton (sandy and mucky, outwash plains, beach ridges, moraines, bogs) second. "The Village of Holly and the most intensively developed portions of the Township, lie within the Marlette-Capac-Houghton soil type and the area of outwash plain."
- Vegetation: beech and maple climax forest; "After the retreat of the glaciers, uplands became forested by oak communities. The kettle depressions developed into tamarack or hardwood swamps, and sometimes remained as lakes surrounded by meadows."
- Character: the township's plan uses the phrase "Up North in Oakland County".
  Source for all of the above: Holly Township Master Plan 2024, pp. 15 to 16, 50, 54 (same URL as above).

## Housing
- Township: "Half of the existing housing stock in Holly Township was constructed before 1990." "Holly Township saw a housing boom between 2000 and 2010, with nearly 40% of Its housing stock being built in that decade." 2,096 housing units in the township.
- Village land use (township plan): a commercial core downtown; pockets of moderate density residential (up to seven units per acre) in five areas; most of the village's edges low density residential (four to five units per acre). Plan goal speaks of "historic single family residential neighborhoods in the Village" and "low density rural residential on acreage" in the township.
  Source: Holly Township Master Plan 2024, pp. 31, 58 to 59, goals section.

## Turf and plant facts used (MSU Extension, read via web.archive.org copies because canr.msu.edu blocks automated reading)
- Mow at 3.5 inches; higher cut builds root mass, crowds out weeds, helps in drought. Raise it in fall too. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/raise_your_mower_height_to_35_inches_to_help_grass_withstand_grub_damage
- "Sandy soils hold less water than loamy soils, so the turf dries out faster in sands." https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/irrigation-practices-to-preserve-water-quality
- "A mat of leaves left on the turf over winter will smother the turf, slow spring green-up and may result in more snow mold." https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/turfgrass-fall-wrap-up-and-snow-mold
- Mulching mowers can handle up to 6 inches of leaves in a pass. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/its_not_too_late_to_mulch_fallen_leaves_into_lawns
- Marcescence: some Michigan trees keep dead leaves through winter, "beech, sycamore, and many types of oak trees". https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/trees_avoid_damage_from_freezing_temperatures_part_1
- Mulch: "Two to three inches of mulch is adequate, more is not better"; no mulch volcano; six inch mulch free ring at the trunk. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/mulch_just_do_it
- Salt: builds up in beds next to sidewalks and driveways; "Sensitive plants like white pines, roses, rhododendrons and yews"; spring rains help flush it. https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/salt_damage_on_landscape_plants
- Wet soil: squeeze test; wait until soil crumbles before working it. MSU soil compaction resources (search result summary, canr.msu.edu/resources/what_to_do_about_compacted_soil). Used only in general terms.
